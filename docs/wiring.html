3V3, not 5V. The PN532 module runs happily at 3.3 V and the XIAO's GPIO is not 5 V tolerant.

Two things that keep the reader alive

Both are optional in the sense that it will probably work without them on the bench, and both are the difference between a drybox that runs for months and one that goes quiet after an hour. If a box keeps dropping its reader, or its reset counter climbs, this card is the fix.

Do thisBecause
100 µF electrolytic + 0.1 µF ceramic across the PN532's VCC and GND, right at the module WiFi TX bursts pull tens of mA. Without local bulk the PN532's rail sags mid‑byte and the frame dies. This is the most common cause.
Keep the two signal wires short — under 10 cm, twisted or ribboned Long dupont jumpers make a good aerial. Route them away from the antenna end of the XIAO.

Wire RSTO. It's how the firmware hard-resets a module that has stopped answering, and it is the only recovery lever there is. Skipping it means a sulking reader stays that way until you power-cycle the box.

Set both DIP switches OFF

Two tiny switches on the PN532. Both off selects HSU (UART), which is the only transport the firmware speaks. Get them wrong and the board is silent — it's the first thing to check if the serial log says Reader 1 init failed.

The pads keep their I²C names. The module prints the I²C pin names on its front and the HSU names on its back — the SDA pad is the module's TX, the SCL pad its RX — and the firmware assigns the board's RX/TX to match. The crossover happens in the pin map, not in the loom, which is why the six wires above go exactly where their labels say.

Keep the wires short. The PN532 gets unreliable past roughly 15 cm of dupont wire. If the reader drops in and out once it's in the drybox, wire length and antenna placement are the first two things to look at — not the firmware.

Pin positions on the PN532 vary between clones — match the silkscreen labels, not the order drawn here. The XIAO's pad layout is exact.
