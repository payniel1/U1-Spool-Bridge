Capacitor Bench Walkthrough

The same retrofit, drawn: what is actually going wrong, where the two parts go, and the order to work through eight boxes.

What goes wrong

The PN532's current has to travel the length of your jumper wires. When the radio takes its bite out of the same rail, the module is left pulling its share through that impedance — and the local voltage at the module, which is the only voltage the PN532 cares about, drops.

Where the two parts go

Both capacitors bridge the same two pins. Nothing else on the module is touched, and your existing six wires stay exactly as they are.

The one that costs you a part

Stripe leg to GND. Backwards, an electrolytic vents — loudly, once, and it can take the module with it. Two markings agree so you can check twice: the printed stripe is the negative side, and on an untrimmed part the negative leg is the shorter one.

How to work through eight boxes

The expensive mistake here is soldering all eight and then finding out the fault was the USB supply. Prove it on one box before committing an afternoon.
